How this is collected

Nothing on this page is clinical guidance.

  • Every morning an automated system reads the HPRA medicine shortage register, the same public information shown on the HPRA website, and stores a dated copy.
  • Weekly, it reads the HPRA List of Interchangeable Medicines, which defines which products the regulator considers substitutable for one another.
  • Each shortage is matched to its interchangeable group by licence number, which is how we can say whether alternatives exist and how many are still available.
  • Healthcare professional letters linked on the register are downloaded once and their publication date is read from the document, so no letter is ever shown without its age.
  • The public register only shows the present day. Because we keep every day's copy, we can also show what appeared, what quietly disappeared, and which return dates changed.
  • What we do not do: we do not access any private data, we do not modify the HPRA's information, and nothing on this page is clinical advice.
